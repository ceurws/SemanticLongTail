# SemanticLongTail — technical report

**Status:** skeleton — technical report intended for Zenodo. Not peer-reviewed.

Working title: The Semantic Long Tail: what 25 years of CEUR-WS volume pages
say beyond their tags.

The CEUR tags of a volume page mark a dozen fields. Everything else on the
page was written by the editors for a human reader and has a meaning by
convention only. The marked fields are the head; the conventions of 25 years
are the tail, thinning out from the affiliation line present in thousands of
volumes to a convention used once. This repository holds the report source
and, as the work proceeds, the scripts, measurements, graph walks and
competency questions it reports on.

- Discussion: [semantify discussion 76](https://github.com/ceurws/semantify/discussions/76)
- Tooling under study: [ceurws/semantify](https://github.com/ceurws/semantify)
- PDF: [main.pdf](main.pdf) (built by CI)

## Research question

Which semantic concepts do 25 years of CEUR-WS volume pages and papers
contain beyond the model we have, what do they mean, and where does naming
them require disambiguation?

The question is open: the concepts are found, not given. New competency
questions arise while the work goes on and the ontology is sharpened with
each concept.

## Method

A loop, not a fixed plan. Each round is recorded as an issue.

1. The residue of a page, what is left after the parser took what it reads,
   is computed over all volumes, clustered and counted. A cluster is a
   candidate concept with its count, its first and last volume and its
   variants.
2. The concept is documented as a [graph walk](https://cr.bitplan.com/index.php/GraphWalk)
   written from real volumes, every node a [PentaLink](https://cr.bitplan.com/index.php/PentaLink)
   with pid, slug, type, kgUrl and webUrl. The walk shows the disambiguation
   needs: a slug with several pids, a slug with no pid, a pid that depends on
   time.
3. The walk produces competency questions, each a named query run against
   the endpoint on ceur-ws.org, so a question is answered on the data.
4. The ontology is sharpened for the concept, with version and date, and the
   record gets the provenance of the field: marked, conventional or deduced.
5. The sharpened ontology changes the residue, and the next concept appears.

## Lessons from the review of SWJ submission 3657-4871

The SemPubFlow paper of 2024 was rejected after nine months in review; the
decisive objections were a demo that was down when one reviewer visited and
results that looked preliminary. The report takes these measures:

- what the readers evaluate is frozen: a tagged release of code, scripts and
  data at submission, and a review instance that runs that tag and nothing
  else, apart from the development server
- the measurements over all volumes are the result; they are reproducible
  from a dated snapshot of the volume pages
- the production state is named with its date in the text
- the model is shown as Turtle and as SPARQL queries first; JSON-LD is an
  RDF serialization and is said so in one sentence

## Method of work

Work is issue driven with agentic AI support: the research question, the
concepts accepted into the model and the conclusions are decided by the
authors; measurements, residue computation, graph walk data and query runs
may be done by agents and are marked per the provenance notation below.

## Provenance notation

Every issue comment carries its origin as a prefix:

| marker | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ✍️🤖 Agent/Name: | generated by the agent |
| 🗣️ Author-Name → 🤖 Agent/Name: | dictated by the human, transcribed and posted by the agent |
| 👁️🧑 Author-Name ✓ | the human read the agent result and confirmed it |
| 👁️🧑 Author-Name ❓ | the human sees an open question |
| 👁️🧑 Author-Name ✗ | the human rejects the result |

Only ✓ counts as confirmed. [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md) lists every agent
contribution to the repository; the Declaration on Generative AI is
finalized by the authors from it.
